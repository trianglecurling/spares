Triangle Curling generally follows, with few exceptions, World Curling Rules of Curling. All members are encouraged to become familiar with these rules and to follow them as amended and implemented for league play. The following material is intended only to address some of the most frequent questions that arise as well as increase the understanding and application of a consistent set of rules across all leagues.

Changes to the WC rules are addressed on an annual basis and this page will be updated accordingly if any further clarification is required, otherwise, rule changes are considered adopted as implemented by World Curling.

This page should be considered as supporting our own League Rules which is an amended version of the WC rules that has been adapted, where necessary, for league play. There are also links in that document to topics on this page.

Unless otherwise noted, all FAQ items pertain to 4-person games.

If you have further questions or would like to see additional FAQ information, please send an email to leaguecoordinators@trianglecurling.com.

What exactly is The Spirit of Curling and how do we apply it?

Here's the definition from the WC Rules of Curling...

"Curling is a game of skill and of tradition. A shot well executed is a delight to see and it is also a fine thing to observe the time-honored traditions of curling being applied in the true spirit of the game. Curlers play to win, but never to humble their opponents. A true curler never attemtps to distract opponents, nor to prevent them from playing their best, and would prefer to lose rather than to win unfairly.

Curlers never knowingly break a rule of the game, nor disrespect any of its traditions. Should they become aware that this has been done inadvertently, they will be the first to divulge the breach.

While the main object of the game of curling is to determine the relative skill of the players, the spirit of curling demands good sportsmanship, kindly feeling and honorable conduct.

This spirit should influence both the interpretation and the application of the rules of the game and the conduct of all participants on and off the ice."

When in doubt about the application of a rule, think first if your interpretation is within the spirit of the game. Some things simply are not covered by the rules (e.g., intentionally burning a rock). Sometimes rules, as originally written by World Curling, are punitive to the point of forfeit and this is not our intent in largely recreational leagues. Fix the problem and move on if you can. Erring on the side of the spirit of curling isn't wrong. We need rules, so that we all play on an equal field, but not to the detriment of our enjoyment of the game. That being said, spirit of curling, doesn't mean to ignore a rule because you don't agree with it. All leagues should be following the rules as outlined in the League Rules with Spirit of Curling judiciously applied so as not to humble the other team.

What is the minimum number of players required to play a game?

Rule 3e

A team may not play with fewer than three players, all players delivering all their allocated stones in each end.

You cannot start a game unless 3 of your players are present and they include spares.

How many players must be from my published roster?

Whether your team is formed by the League Coordinator, skips' draw, make-your-own, or some combination thereof, your team's roster is published on the website at the begnning of each season. One (1) person from the published team must be present at the game or it is a forfeit. If such a forfeit situation is identified in advance of your game, the forfeiting team should still make every effort to find a replacement team to play in their stead so that the other team still gets a chance to play their allocated number of games, or possibily pick up a spare opportunity with another team in the draw.

If that person is late, the game can still begin (see below for I was late for my game...). If they have not arrived after the conclusion of 2 ends, the game is a forfeit.

In the case where longer-term sparing may be due to injury or sabbatical replacements, the spare should be considered part of the team's roster instead of the replaced person for determining the number of published players present.

In our club, the Leads do the coin toss. In other clubs, it's the Vices. Which is correct?

There is no rule about who must do the coin toss. In fact, a coin toss is not the only option for who gets the hammer in the first end (check out Last Stone Draw in the World Curling rules as an example). Our club convention is to have the Leads do it which is why in other clubs it may be different. Anyone can do the toss and if there is someone from each team present, you can even get it done in the warm room beforehand. This helps ensure your game gets started on time and makes maximum use of your ice time.

What is the difference between throwing order and functions on a team?

Great question! So much so that World Curling changed the terminology starting with the 2025/26 season, replacing the word "position" with "function" to clear up confusion on this point. Throwing order is pretty much exactly how it sounds - who throws in what order. Typically, you will hear the players referred to as Lead, Second, Third and Fourth or similar. Functions (Skip and Vice) are independent of throwing order but as with throwing order, must be maintained by the same people throughout a game (except as subject to rules that cover injury). The Skip and Vice are in charge of the house and though they are often the Third and Fourth in throwing order, there is no requirement that they throw in that order. Only the Skip and Vice can sweep an opponent's stone and only they may be past the hog line at the playing end when the other team is throwing.

Can we change our throwing order or assigned functions (Skip/Vice) during a game?

Our implementation of rule 3.b

"A team's delivery rotation and its skip and vice-skip functions are determined by play in the first end and it maintains that rotation and those functions throughout the game subject to d.II. A team that DELIBERATELY changes its delivery rotation, or functions, during a game, will forfeit the game. If a team ACCIDENTALLY changes its delivery rotation, or functions, during a game, they must correct the error in the following end without penalty and continue to maintain the original order and functions. Any SUBSEQUENT incident in that game by the same team, shall result in a forfeit by that team."

So, the short answer is "no." Anticipate that any issue with throwing order or roles is unintentional and fix it in the next end. Also understand that sometimes it may be necessary and acceptable if someone has to take charge of the house if the Skip or Vice has to leave the ice for a short period of time. World Curling inserted a new rule (R4(b)) to clarify this very point in 2025.

I was late for my game and they already started. What should I do?

Our implementation of rule 3.c

"If a player is missing at the start of a game, the team may either:

  1. start the game with three players, the first two players delivering three stones each, and the third player delivering two stones, in which case the missing player may enter the game in the delcared delivery rotation and position at the start of an end; or
  2. start the game using an alternate at the discretion of the League Coordinator. The alternate must play in the throwing order and position of the missing player and in case of their return, the missing player must then replace the alternate at the same position and throwing order at the beginning of an end."

Unless there is a declared alternate (this would be exceptionally rare), we will follow rule 3.c.I. You will have to wait until the next end and the position in which you enter will become your "declared" throwing position. You will also have to wait to sweep until the start of the next end. Teams should, therefore, carefully consider whether they want to wait a few minutes for you to get on the ice or start without you.

What happens if there's an injury during a game or a person is otherwise unable to continue?

Our implementation of rule 3.d

"Where a player is unable to continue to play in a game, the team may either:

  1. continue to play with the remaining three players, in which case the player who left the game may re-enter only at the start of an end. A player may leave and return to a game only one time in any game; or
  2. bring in an alternate at the beginning of an end, at the discretion of the League Coordinator, without change to the rotation or positions, and the replaced player may not re-enter the game."

An available alternate being a rare exception, we will typically follow 3.d.I, noting the following... In the event the skip becomes injured, the vice will become the skip. If the vice is injured or replacing the skip, you may choose from among the remaining two players to assume the vice position. Throwing order remains the same. In the unfortunate event of a second injury to the same team, the game is forfeit.

What happens if a player is unable to deliver one or both of their stones but can still sweep?

This is a very uncommon situation, but it is covered by rules 3.g and 3.h. Consult those for how throwing order is affected.

Is it ok to stand outside of the hog lines when my team is not delivering a stone?

The rules are quite specific on this and the answer is that you MUST stand between the hog lines with exceptions for the skip and the vice on the playing end and the person delivering the next stone on the delivery end.

Our implementation of rule 4.a

  1. Non-Delivering Team:
    1. During the process of delivery, the players take stationary positions along the sidelines between the hog lines. However:
      1. the skip and/or vice-skip may take stationary positions behind the back line at the playing end, but must not interfere with the choice of place of the skip or the vice-skip of the delivering team.
      2. the player who is to deliver next may take a stationary position to the side of the sheet, behind the hack, at the delivery end.
    2. The non-delivering team players must not take any position, nor cause any motion, which could obstruct, interfere with, distract, or intimidate the delivering team. If such an action occurs, or an external force distracts a player during delivery, that player has the option of allowing the play to stand, or of redelivering the stone after all displaced stones have been replaced to their positions prior to the violation taking place.

So, this is not just curling etiquette, it's an actual rule and is one of the most often broken. ONLY the skip and/or vice should be in the house when your team is not delivering and the next player to throw can be off to the side behind the hack. That's it. And remember, skip and vice are roles, not throwing order (it's not automatically third and fourth in the order). Anyone else MUST stand between the hog lines. In the Spirit of Curling, if you need a tissue or a drink, or any other reason you should be where you're not supposed to be, then take a second to let the other skip know so it doesn't become an issue.

One of the things that most people don't realize is that there are remedies for this and that's covered above in a.II. If you're not where you're supposed to be or in any way a distraction to the opposing team, they have the right to redeliver their stone if they so desire.

If you need to have a chat with all of your teammates in the middle of a game, just make sure to do it between the hog lines at the sideline.

When can I sweep behind the tee line at the playing end?

Only one person from each team can sweep behind the tee line at the playing end. If you are NOT the delivering team and sweep the opponent's stone, then it can ONLY be the skip or vice. This is usually not an issue if the other two players of the non-delivering team are standing between the hog lines as they are supposed to. Note that each team can sweep one stone (theirs or the opponent's) and if they stop sweeping that stone, any eligible player from the team can sweep another rock if desired.

Allowed: any player of the delivering team

  • Yellow team is delivering and wants to remove red's rock in the top 12. Weight is light, but contact is made. Anyone, but only one person from the throwing (yellow) team can sweep red's rock once it reaches the tee line.

Not allowed: only skip or vice of the non-delivering team

  • Red and yellow have two rocks sitting about a foot apart in the top 4. Yellow attempts to take out red's rock but overcurls and hits their own rock. The second from the red team jumps in and sweeps yellow's rock once it touches the tee line. This is not allowed! Only the skip or the vice of the red (non-throwing) team can sweep yellow's rock behind the tee line.

2 minutes for obstructing!

Rule 9.g

"Behind the tee line, a team has first priviledge of sweeping its own stone, but it must not obstruct or prevent its opponent from sweeping."

Most often, in the case of obstruction, the offending skip is not aware of what it is they are doing to cause the issue, but here are some that have been witnessed at our club and others:

  • Swinging your broom as the shot is coming into the house
  • Placing a broom horizontally across your chest
  • Sticking a leg out while squatting down
  • Trying to get in between the other skip and the rock when their position has already been established

While these may not be intentional, they are nevertheless some examples of obstruction and skips should be developing some spatial awareness of their environment to prevent their occurrence. Though the skip sweeping their own stone has first right of access to their stone, if they do not sweep until the other skip has already started sweeping it, the opposing skip is under no obligation give up their "first" position next to the rock. As with all rocks behind the tee line, one rock at a time for both teams please!

I heard a story about a skip yelling out to burn a rock. Is that kosher?

If there was ever an action that egregiously violates the Spirit of Curling, this would be it. Consider that the stone your teammate just threw is about to destroy your carefully set up house. Wouldn't you like to prevent that by having your sweepers intentionally burn the rock before it crosses the hog line? That way, only that stone comes out of play.

Technically, there is no rule that prevents you from doing it and it is a hot topic of discussion among rules officials. But it is definitely not kosher. The reason brush heads had to change in sanctioned competition was because sweepers were having too great an effect on the shot and discounting the ability of the thrower. The same can be said of teams that would consider intentionally burning a rock. Burning a rock is supposed to be accidental, not to purposely affect shot outcomes.

All we have left, unless rule changes are implemented, is the Spirit of Curling but SoC is not an enforceable rule. Act accordingly and never intentionally burn a rock on your own or by someone else's insistence. If it happens against you, 1) keep in mind it needs to be pretty obvious and if so then 2) politely inform the opposing skip that it isn't acceptable. Though we don't anticipate this actually ever happening at Triangle (and shudder to even bring it up), repeated violations should be dealt with by your League Coordinator.

What is the "No Tick Shot" rule and do we use it?

Rule 7

"If prior to the delivery of the sixth stone of an end, a delivered stone causes either directly or indirectly, an opposition stone in the Free Guard Zone (FGZ) which is touching the center line to be moved to an off-center line position or to a position outside the FGZ, the non-offending team has the option to:

  1. Remove the delivered stone from play, and replace all stones that were displaced to their positions prior to the violation taking place; or
  2. Leave all stones where they came to rest. If a stone is moved from the center line to an out-of-play position, then the FGZ rule R6.b applies."

This rule was first introduced at the competitive level as the non-hammer team would place a center guard and the hammer team would, in many cases, just tick it to the corner, removing its effectiveness and increasing their own (two corner guards). Most clubs did not immediately adopt this rule as execution of a tick strategy is somewhat difficult to execute without potentially removing the center guard from the FGZ and just ending up having to replace it. It is gradually gaining acceptance at clubs and in bonspiels, and Triangle will be enforcing this rule in all leagues (except Doubles where it does not apply) as of Fall 2025.

If there is any doubt about whether a stone prior to the sixth stone of an end is on the center line, make sure that doubt is resolved by measurement if necessary (use the square) prior to delivery of the next stone. Also note, this rule applies equally to both team's stones, not just those of the non-hammer team and that, as with FGZ rules, it is ok to move your own stone off the center line.

What if I the delivered stone hits another rock into one on the center line and it moves it off of center?

Wheter the cause is direct (the delivered stone) or indirect (caused by the delivered stone contacting another stone which in turn moves the center line stone off center), the result is the same and the rule has been violated.

What if the stone on the center line is moved but not completely off the line or out of the FGZ?

In this case, the rule has not been violated and the stones remain where they rest. No action is necessary.

Is a stone touching a stone on the center line but not itself on the center line still considered to be touching the line?

No. If that stone is moved, it's ok. Only the stone actually on the center line matters.

If I'm certain a delivered stone is going to hit the center line rock and move it, can either team just stop it and leave the rocks where they are?

No. That is intentionally burning a stone and it is not ok. Even if the opposing skip is ok with it, this is never a good idea. Let the shot play out and give the opposing skip the opportunity to decide whether to leave the stones as they came to rest or to replace everything and remove the delivered stone.

What is "warming the ice" and is it allowed?

Warming the ice is sweeping an area of the ice to melt the pebble in anticipation of a rock's arrival at that location to get that rock to move farther and is a sweeping violation associated with two rules.

Rule 8.b

"A stationary stone must be set in motion before it can be swept. A stone set in motion by a delivered stone, either directly, or indirectly, may be swept by any one or more of the team to which it belongs anywhere in front of the tee line at the playing end."

Rule 8.e

"No player may ever sweep an opponent's stone except behind the tee line at the playing end and may not start to sweep an opponent's stone until it has reached the tee line at the playing end."

An example

During a bonspiel, a skip started sweeping behind a rock of theirs sitting stationary in the house, well ahead of the arrival of the incoming rock that would contact it but was not at sufficient weight to move the stationary one to where they wanted it to be. When challenged on this, they claimed that it was their own rock and therefore could do with it as they pleased. Simply put, it is never ok to sweep a stationary rock, regardless of who it belongs to.

There's no penalty for this, so the only option you have is to remind the other skip that it is not legal.

Concessions, arithmetic eliminations, and ties

Rule 12(a)

"The result of a game is decided by a majority of points at the completion of the scheduled ends of play, or when a team concedes victory to its opponent, or when one team is arithmetically eliminated, provided the minimum number of ends has been completed. A team that has been arithmetically eliminated may finish the current end, but no new end may be started. However, if a team is arithmetically eliminated in the last end of a game, the game must stop when this occurs, and the end will not be finished. If the score is tied at the completion of the scheduled ends, play continues with the extra end(s) and the team that scores first wins the game."

Because of the way we are time-constrained in leagues, we implement this rule with some differences.

Minimum number of ends
While there is no ninimum number of ends that must be played at Triangle, six (6) are suggested so members get maximum value for their ice time unless a game is so lopsided as to not be enjoyable. This is entirely up to the playing teams to decide (up to 8 ends of course).
Concessions
If you are conceding your game, please do so officially. Offer your hand to the other skip. If you do not, and continue to play for fun, and the conceding team ends up scoring enough points to win the game, though it would not be in the spirit of curling, you would still technically be able to claim the win. Make sure the concession is offered and acknowledged. All team members should shake hands at that point, regardless of any continued use of the ice after that.
Arithmetic eliminations
If you reach a point where you are arithmetically eliminated (not enough stones left to at least tie the game, your game is over. Finish the end if it is anything but the last available end, but stop if you're in the last end. All should shake hands and it needs to be the eliminated team's skip that offers without exception.
Ties
We don't have time for extra ends so "Skip's" rocks determine the winner. Any person from the published roster of each team can be selected to deliver a single stone and closest to the pin wins the game (as measured with the laser). You may not select a spare to throw the tie-breaking rock. Offensive sweeping only.
A note on continued play
If time permits and your league supports it, you may continue to use the ice after conceding or having been arithmetically eliminated. However, please be mindful of any league which follows you and yield the ice appropriately. In no case should your use of the ice extend beyond your scheduled time, and in the event you are the last team on the ice, please be considerate of any later league and cede the ice at least 5 minutes before your scheduled end time to give them a few extra minutes to get started.
